> Religion [...] consists of the belief that there is an unseen order, and our supreme good lies in harmoniously adjusting ourselves thereto.”

― **William James, [The Varieties of Religious Experience](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/1751058)**

Do we walk this life alone, encountering the challenges and promises of a quiet cosmos? Or do we exist in the light of a divine will?

Let’s first acknowledge that there’s no getting away from our [scientific-material orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysical_naturalism) of the cosmos. The way we view the world, whether we look up at the stars in darkness away from city lights or envision how our life might play out and how it sits within the web of other lives and distant events, we adopt a scientific and historical view. We construct the best story we can against the evidence and facts as we understand them.

So how does God and religious belief fit into this? Typically it’s either brought in as an entirely separable conceptual space, i.e. God exists beyond this world, though _He_ may intervene in it. Heaven and hell, and all other numinous notions are immaterial and apart from the material.

Increasingly of course our aggressively agnostic, atheistic, or perhaps most prominently the [material](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientistic_materialism) outlook tends toward a godless, [chaos theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaos_theory#:~:text=Chaos%20theory%20is,disorder%20and%20irregularities) inspired view of the cosmos, i.e. its random and meaningless. Individual events have only shallow meaning to those who it impacts. Potential universals like good, evil, and pain are simply locally experienced phenomena up for a flurry of interpretations.

# Modern Mayhem

Where does this leave the modern truth seeker? Unfortunately it leaves us, as cognitive scientist John Vervaeke described, in a [meaning crisis](https://www.meaningcrisis.co/the-introduction-to-the-meaning-crisis-series/). Scrolling through TikTok to find the image sequence that suffices to quell the deeper questions and offer reprieve from a general sense of unmooring. We may see it in the increasing numbers of young people who feel isolated and alone, the growing number of those willing to take their own life.

Religion, even when we have to deal with a handful of various faiths, offers a common frame of reference for morality, norms, conventions, and individual sense making within a broader social context. Few social movements were able to bring about progress, from emancipation and civil rights to women’s suffrage, without the language of faith. Society generally functions not through rigid oversight and expertly quantified incentives but through ethical obligations, people working when no credit is available. Science itself relies on a myriad ethical norms and offers relatively scant tangible reward or recognition. Increasing levels of social fragmentation and partisanship are likewise consequences.

# The Word in the Age of Science

To resolve this we don’t need a categorical shift in our language or cosmic understanding, we simply need to iterate our perspective based on our latest scientific view of the world.

We perceive the world according to material, fact-based prior beliefs. Yet our conscious experience and humanity’s unique place in the cosmos provide evidence for the divine. Specifically not for God as a separate entity, but for what I call Godness and numinous materialism. Science supports the sacred, partly through logic but partly through logic’s necessary rhetorical and cognitive correspondent, analogic or figurative reasoning. We can take our understanding of the constructive process of reality and at least informatively speculate, if not rationally arrive at, a coherent view of cosmic nature.

NM follows in the tradition of Allan Watts, in a similar vein to John Vervaeke, while extending it through an information processing and active inference framework. It is the view that modern metaphysics suggests the existence of divinity, but not God as an isolated and separable entity wrapped in archaic archetypes. Rather, the divine exists as _Godness_. The nature of the permeable and interconnected cosmic reality we all jointly inhabit is not a quiet and cold universe populated with separable parts indifferent to the whole.

The presence of life, consciousness, and in particular humanity’s moral agency, self-consciousness, meta-cognition and symbolic capacity, suggest that we inhabit a unified cognitive omniscience that lies both beyond individual or even group conscious understanding but within which we contribute and participate. Godness is simply a more accurate description of the inherently numinous understanding of reality, i.e. one that extends through space and time and encompasses all those physical concepts within it.

My [myth of objectivity hypothesis](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fsoc.2023.1269621/abstract) (MOH) proposes that what makes humans unique is our ability, founded originally in moral agency and typing, to dance through the more dynamic nature of duality. Dual self/other boundaries exist in perception so, it seems a trickster god visited our ancestors to suggest: _why not shift your perception?_ If people can believe themselves as part of the group, or only part of their own part, then what are the limits of their boundaries?

The nature of the cosmos as extrapolated from life and human symbolic experience suggests a kind of parsimonious paradox, between the creation of the dual barriers between the world and individual, and the view of reality as inherently unified and non-dual. Note this paradox may be resolved when we see one as pragmatic and the other as ontological, i.e. truthful, yet the paradox I believe persists. How much confidence can we possess of these objective concept delineations? What is ontology but our own perception projected in some shared imaginary frame of reference? The paradox is that our ability for general apprehension is made possible by defining boundaries, yet the extent of our understanding is determined by the ability to cross over them as inherently permeable.

# Pillars of the Numinous

There are four important features of our modern understanding of reality that suggest core insights of religion are true.

Firstly, we need to build a justifiable and coherent metaphysics. To do this we can identify any number of modern scientific domains that ground biology in the important physical attributes including information-theory and thermodynamics. Such as systems thinking (as Dennis Noble does for Biology), information processing theory, the view of life as the computation or communication (as Chris fields does for physics), and finally, and most succinctly I believe, the Free Energy Principle (FEP), i.e. the view of life as predictive modeling and minimizing uncertainty against probabilistic beliefs (as Karl Friston does for generalizing the view of cognition to physical principles). That independent paradigms in biology, physics, and neuroscience converge on information-theoretic principles suggests a useful way to understand how biology operates.

Secondly, these scientific approaches support nondual monism, the idea that there is one underlying reality and different perspective or models of it, that duality is a pragmatic construction that shifts, the seer is not a fixed object but multitudinous in nature. Rejecting the prevalent view of both religion and science in our modern, Western (or WEIRD) world.

Thirdly, is what we can see as hierarchical integration and scaling of living systems. This is at the heart of conscious experience, and intelligence. Life develops through a process of integrating a variety of local perspectives into broader coordinations. From oragenelles integrating into a cell, from cells integrating into tissues and organs of complex agents, from organisms into a society, and finally to human culture. This trajectory can be understood in informational terms, the evolution and development of life involves greater computational modeling that processes and distributes local insights into an increasing global scheme.

Finally, this leads to the true evidence of religion which follows from the human experience. My Transcendental Model Selection (TMS) formalizes our ability to cognitively scale through language and culture. We can model the entire world and our own identity as a mutable part of it.

This connects to the modern psychological treatment of transcendence. This claims it is precisely transcending the experience of the local isolation and separation into the inherent interconnectedness that is at the heart of what William James called the religious experience. We integrate our perspective into a broader view of the world, become less attached to our myopic points of view and recognize that we are part of a greater system. This follows from Scott Barry Kaufmann’s work.

# From Reductive Dualism to Parsimonious Modeling

Recently, Ross Douthat’s book _Believe_ elides the religious tendency to either ignore or confront the scientific worldview and offers a compelling option to integrate numinous truth with the scientific. But critically he fails on metaphysical grounds. He veers too closely to the (ontological) [reductionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reductionism#:~:text=Ren%C3%A9%20Descartes%2C%20in%20De%20homine%20\(1662\)%2C%20claimed%20that%20non%2Dhuman%20animals%20could%20be%20explained%20reductively%20as%20automata%3B%20meaning%20essentially%20as%20more%20mechanically%20complex%20versions%20of%20this%20Digesting%20Duck.) and its influence on many Western (or WEIRD) religious traditions in offering a dualistic God that stands apart from and in judgment of individual souls.

As mentioned above, to ground the metaphysical view that I believe is most clarifying we can begin with information processing theory and Karl Friston’s Free Energy Principle (FEP). Why?

Firstly the FEP is primarily interested in statistically modeling actual cognition and often representing this practically in-silico, i.e. for AI. It is not overburdened by theory or abstractions. What does this perspective find? It does not find an a priori, dualistic dividxe between the Self and other. Instead it sees agency as an ongoing exchange of information that includes the construction of perceptual divides between the self and the world.

An important element of this predictive architecture is its hierarchical and networked organization. The cognitive processes of complex organisms involve predictions at higher levels that cascade downward and lower levels that make sensory observations and pass errors associated with those predictions back up. This gives rise to agency itself, the ability of say, an organism to fulfill particular objectives or goals through the integration of its lower level parts.

Broadly, a given level of intelligence and consciousness may be equated with the breadth and depth of the size of a given network and the tightness of its integration. A hive can accomplish more than a single bee and may have a sort of intelligence yet their individual cognition does not meet the general problem solving and teleological capacity of a chimp or a bonobo. Likewise the integration of human culture, due to its symbolic and anonymous information processing and cultural exchange, exceeds the designs of an elephant herd. This speaks to the theme of the many gaining power through one, individual information processing brought to scale produces intelligence, consciousness, etc.

The FEP tends to view consciousness as an attempt to model and distinguish agency, i.e. as an uncertainty minimizing agent that needs to be cognizant of predictions or expectations in order to persist in an environment of nondiminishing or increasing uncertainty, it behooves me to understand the casual forces that influence my predictive capacity. This, I argue, can be extended to humanity’s unique consciousness beginning with moral agency, characterized as the inference of a neutral and common yet effervescent cultural frame of reference using (initially) moral beliefs (gossip, admonishment, etc.). I need to understand whether your beliefs suggest an expected normative pattern that can allow us to potentially cooperate or peaceably inhabit a shared environment.

My [MOH](https://exhibitingbias.netlify.app/) and its formal presentation in [TMS](https://exhibitingbias.netlify.app/#:~:text=the%20AGI%20connection-,Transcendental%20Model%20Selection,-DEEPENING%20AND%20INTEGRATING) suggests that, as humans, moral agency is central to our unique ability to see ourselves and others as part of a shared and connected Self. Critically it represents a rapid scaling and integration across time, space, and other people and perspectives. Stating a belief explicitly by declaring that the murder of an innocent child was wrong or implicitly by intervening when we notice a theft signals a predictable identity to any observer, intimate or otherwise. This involves the continual construction of common or overlapping identities with strangers and others we associate with culturally or through things like religious belief.

This minimal form of objectivity is necessary for language and symbolic communication and thought (i.e. I need to implicitly believe that these words have meaning beyond my own and other’s perception), but this practical use I believe speaks to a greater metaphysical truth, individual qualia, subjective experience. The story that we each inhabit a separable being is how we experience the ups and downs of existence yet constitutes an embodied “playful” illusion. I’m playfully “Shaggy” a person who maintains continuity despite the myriad of developmental and social changes that marked my life history, and I can contextualize myself at any given moment along numerous lines, lines that can broadly be described along narrow/broadness.

As life in general involves the construction of lines between the Self and others, to facilitate the information exchange of the cosmos, human perspective in particular points towards a universal truth that these processes are part of a larger and interconnected hierarchy of predictive modeling. An implicit omniscience that subsumes our experience of individual agency is most evident in our quest to find meaning by adopting a cultural orientation of the divine or numinous.

# Ancient Wisdom:{Modern Stat Jargon}

We can extend the insights of Allan Watts’ emphasis on the [Daoist notion of Wu-Wei](https://share.google/899Ar4zuTTcvsc1ff) spontaneity and the Hindu notion of Maya playfulness while traveling further from John Vervaeke’s [ecology of practices](https://awakentomeaning.com/vfeop/) and cognitive sense making to see that this world is the stuff of providential intention.

The “weak form” of cultural perspective we necessarily adopt for language connects directly to the stronger forms of beliefs in deities or higher power found in religion. The most obvious connections are to the nondualistic traditions of Hinduism, Buddhism, and Daoism. Where the participatory immersion in what active inference describes as the modeling process of life syncs most obviously. However even the abrahamic traditions, while often reduced to dogmatic beliefs, moral codes, rituals, etc. may at the core speak to this same metaphysical pronouncement of Numinous materialism, i.e. the idea of the “kingdom of God” being within each person, and the ultimate the story of the [Book of Job](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Job) and its emphasis on our ability to understand the complex workings of God and the cosmos.

Our ancestors, those that developed symbolic capacity and inhabited cultural systems I believe adopted numinous materialism intuitively. They likely experienced life without modern feelings of alienation, anxiety, and existential confusions. This follows from transcendental inference and model selection, the attempt to formalize our myth of objectivity in the active inference predictive hierarchy. It demonstrates how humanity uniquely models an infinitely complex cultural environment by integrating the breadth and depth of our models our a perceived cultural perspective, i.e. a divine eye that can “objectively” transcends our individual perceptions. The ancients employed notions of deities and the divine generally to ground their own lives under a stable perspective. Though the worship of polytheistic deities and the rivalries that emerged between groups and factions within groups may seem to us parochial, the trajectory of faith often widens our moral circle, our conceptual Self rather than narrowing it.

# Living Material Truth

The author David Foster Wallace, in his famous [valedictorian address](https://shaggy.substack.com/p/the-water-in-our-stories-and-reality), intuited the insights of John Vervaeke that religion guides what are otherwise [maladaptive, anxiety provoking](https://dismantledmind.com/parasitic-processing/), and harmful beliefs. Religion changes the stories we tell ourselves, not just through explicit conscious propositions but through the stories we intuit in our “gut.” It enables us to act in concert, or concordance to a broader or greater self than we otherwise would. Our words, our thoughts, our stories can focus on narrow aims of our personal needs and ambitions. Aims that are not only socially harmful but psychologically isolating. Broadening our sights, elevating our designs is precisely the object of religious practice.

God should not be something that is delivered eloquently by some authority or even follow a logical conclusion. That is because our conscious thoughts can only attend to a small portion of the much greater information our bodies can implicitly and intuitively process. The main thrust of religion is making use of institutionalized practices that guide this “wider net” of knowing such that we feel the truth of religion in our daily lives, acting both more prosaically and avoiding anxiety and depressive feelings.

However the largest truth of religion, for my purpose here at least, is to offer a more truthful perspective than the one our minds, our natural biases tend towards. This is that our individual, personal, or generally parochial/intimate group pursuits should be understood as part of a greater cosmic perspective.

We can see the ancient truth embedded in religion in its language. Sin etymologically may originate in an archer “missing their mark.” The source of sin is not a lack of personal will or even selfishly bad priorities but in misunderstanding. It syncs well with information processing and the idea that the stuff of life involves integrating and scaling local insights into ever broader designs.

This aligns with the view of FEP and broad notions of 4E cognition, our bodies are continually processing sensory data from our natural, social, and cultural environment according to its own model, or probabilistic prior beliefs. What does this imply about the truth? About the truth that our conscious minds can feasibly understand?

This is Numinous materialism, i.e that the divine line shines through our physical, our biological experience. We can uniquely construct a personal, subjective narrative of a mind and experience separate from those around us, our culture, and the cosmos writ large.
